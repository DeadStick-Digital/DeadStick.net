Overview

DeadStick Utilities is device care for people who read the fine print. On the Mac it reviews cleanup candidates, maps storage, inventories applications, and watches system health without becoming a performance problem itself. On iPhone and iPad it works strictly within the platform's rules: photo cleanup reports, contact backup and review, and storage guidance for the surfaces Apple actually allows apps to touch.

The design premise is that a utility with the power to delete your files should be held to a higher standard than a marketing page. Every destructive action is review-first: you see what was found, why it was flagged, what will happen, and how to undo it. Cleanup moves files to a local quarantine with a restore record before anything is permanently removed.

What it does

  • Review-first Mac cleanup with explanation, local quarantine, and restore paths — nothing is deleted blind.
  • Storage map that shows where space goes, from source to review posture to a recoverable outcome.
  • Read-only application inventory, with uninstall and leftovers workflows planned for the Pro tier behind safety evidence.
  • Mac health monitoring engineered for low overhead — the app reports its own CPU and memory footprint on the same screen.
  • iPhone and iPad photo cleanup reports: screenshots, large media, and strict duplicates backed by exact evidence, with final review through the Photos Recently Deleted recovery path.
  • Contact backup and export before any merge review — backup first is enforced, not suggested.
  • A privacy and network screen that shows the app's endpoint posture; the default build has no app-owned endpoints to show.
  • Free core features, with a planned DeadStick Utilities Pro tier at $9.99/year for automation capabilities after release evidence exists.

How your data is protected

DeadStick Utilities is local-first by default: no account, no analytics SDK, no advertising, and no app-owned cloud processing. Scan results, cleanup history, and quarantine records stay on the device. Diagnostics are opt-in and the support export is redacted, previewed, and saved only where you choose — nothing is transmitted automatically.

The app also refuses the dark patterns common in this category: no fake "your device is infected" warnings, no red-alert theatrics to sell an upgrade, and no system-junk cleaning claims on iPhone or iPad that the platform does not actually permit.

Built to government-grade assurance practices

DeadStick Utilities is designed to meet or exceed applicable government-grade software assurance practices, and each practice below maps to a maintained evidence artifact in the product's release process. No agency approval, certification, or formal validation is claimed — the point is that the evidence exists and ships with every release.

  • NIAP-informed design. The product maintains a mapping of its architecture against NIAP Protection Profile requirements for application software, kept current as features land.
  • NIST SSDF secure development. Development follows a NIST SP 800-218 Secure Software Development Framework checklist that is reviewed and updated with every release cycle.
  • FIPS-aware cryptography posture. No custom cryptography, ever. Sensitive-data protection uses Apple platform cryptographic services, and the exact crypto posture is documented in a standing statement.
  • SBOM with every release. A software bill of materials is a release gate: a build that cannot produce its SBOM does not ship.
  • Section 508 / WCAG accessibility evidence. A VPAT/ACR track is maintained, and automated accessibility and WCAG contrast gates run against the actual UI as part of validation.
  • Local-only and MDM deployment guide. Managed environments get a documented local-only posture, including managed-policy controls that tighten the app without changing its architecture.
  • Documented network endpoints. The endpoint inventory is a tracked artifact. The default build's inventory is empty, and any future endpoint must be declared, justified, and reviewed before it exists.
  • Signed and notarized releases. Public Mac builds are signed and notarized, with build evidence recorded against a release-gate checklist before distribution.
  • Reversibility as a requirement. Destructive actions require preview, explanation, and an undo or quarantine path wherever the platform makes one technically possible.

Honest platform limits

iOS and iPadOS do not let third-party apps clean system caches or "boost RAM," so DeadStick Utilities does not pretend to. Mobile cleanup is scoped to Photos, Contacts, Files you select, and storage guidance — the surfaces Apple provides. On the Mac, Pro workflows like app uninstall and leftovers cleanup stay locked until their safety evidence passes, even for paying customers. Software that can touch your data should earn each capability.
